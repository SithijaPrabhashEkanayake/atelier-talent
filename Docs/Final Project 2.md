### **CHAPTER 1 – INTRODUCTION**

#### **1.1 Chapter Overview**

This chapter introduces the research by presenting the background of the study, identifying the research problem, defining the research aim and objectives, formulating the research questions, describing the scope and significance of the study, and outlining the overall thesis structure. It establishes the rationale for developing a centralized digital platform to support global talent recruitment within the fashion, entertainment, and pageantry industries.

#### **1.2 Background of the Study**

Information and Communication Technology (ICT) has changed the way organizations communicate, conduct business, and recruit employees. Today, digital platforms help employers connect with talented people from different parts of the world. Technologies such as cloud computing, mobile applications, artificial intelligence, and secure authentication have made recruitment faster, easier, and more efficient.

The fashion and entertainment industries have grown rapidly over the past few years. Fashion shows, advertisements, films, digital content, modeling competitions, and beauty pageants create many job opportunities worldwide. As these industries continue to grow, there is a greater need for better recruitment systems.

In the past, modeling agencies helped connect models with clients. They managed portfolios, negotiated contracts, and guided models in their careers. Today, many models work independently and use social media platforms like Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and LinkedIn to promote themselves. Although these platforms provide good exposure, they are not designed for professional recruitment. Recruiters often find it difficult to verify profiles, check experience, and identify suitable candidates.

The lack of a centralized recruitment platform creates many challenges. Models struggle to find genuine job opportunities. Recruiters must search through different websites and social media accounts to find suitable talent. Fake profiles, fraudulent casting calls, and poor communication also reduce trust in the recruitment process.

Industry professionals such as fashion brands, modeling agencies, photographers, film directors, commercial directors, and event organizers also face difficulties when searching for qualified talent. Most recruitment is still done through manual portfolio reviews, agency referrals, or social media. These methods are often slow and inefficient.

7

Pageant organizers face similar problems. They need an organized system to manage applications, communicate with contestants, and conduct recruitment. However, many organizers still rely on emails, social media posts, and manual registration, which increases their workload.

Existing platforms solve only some of these problems. LinkedIn mainly supports corporate recruitment, while Backstage and StarNow focus on specific areas of the entertainment industry. Most of these platforms do not provide one secure system that connects models, recruiters, and pageant organizers. They also lack advanced search, profile verification, and country-based talent discovery features.

Algorithm-based talent matching can further improve the recruitment process by automatically comparing the requirements of a casting opportunity with the characteristics of available talents. The proposed system can consider factors such as age, height, location, experience, category, skills, and portfolio information when identifying suitable candidates. A weighted matching approach can be used to calculate a suitability score for each talent and rank candidates according to their compatibility with a particular casting requirement. This can reduce the time required for manual candidate searching and help recruiters identify relevant talents more efficiently.

The proposed system can also use search and filtering algorithms to support recruiters in finding suitable talents based on specific requirements. Instead of manually reviewing a large number of profiles, recruiters can enter relevant criteria and receive a filtered list of candidates. These algorithm-based approaches can improve the efficiency, consistency, and usability of the recruitment process.

Therefore, there is a need for a specialized digital platform that combines professional talent profiles, portfolio management, casting management, advanced search and filtering, and algorithm-based talent matching within a single system. The proposed Global Multidimensional Talent Marketplace and Casting Management System aims to address these limitations by providing a centralized and structured environment for models, industry professionals, and pageant organizers.

#### **2.2 Talent Marketplace Systems**

Talent marketplace systems are digital platforms that connect individuals with organizations seeking specialized skills and professional services. These platforms enable users to create professional profiles, upload portfolios, search for opportunities, and communicate directly with recruiters.

The rapid growth of digital technologies has significantly transformed recruitment processes. Traditional hiring methods relied heavily on physical applications, paper-based portfolios, and face-to-face interviews. Modern digital platforms have improved recruitment by reducing geographical barriers, increasing accessibility, and providing faster communication between employers and applicants.

20

Research by Kumar and Lee (2020) suggests that successful digital talent marketplaces generally consist of three core components: user profile management, intelligent search capabilities, and communication mechanisms. Together, these components improve recruitment efficiency while expanding employment opportunities across international markets.

Search and filtering capabilities are particularly important in talent marketplaces because recruiters often need to identify candidates according to specific requirements. Filtering users based on characteristics such as location, experience, skills, and professional category can reduce the number of unsuitable candidates that require manual evaluation.

Although general talent marketplace systems perform effectively for conventional recruitment, they rarely address the specialized requirements of the modeling, entertainment, and pageant industries. Professional modeling recruitment requires visual portfolio management, appearancebased filtering, verification processes, and structured casting workflows, which are generally absent from conventional employment platforms.

Therefore, a specialized talent marketplace can extend the concepts of existing digital recruitment systems by combining professional profiles, multimedia portfolios, advanced search, casting management, and algorithm-based talent matching within a single platform.

#### **2.3 Casting Management Systems in the Modeling Industry**

Casting management systems are specialized recruitment platforms designed for selecting models, actors, performers, and contestants for fashion events, advertising campaigns, television productions, films, and beauty pageants.

Traditional casting procedures continue to depend heavily on modeling agencies, manual portfolio reviews, and physical auditions (Nikolaou, 2021). While these approaches allow recruiters to evaluate candidates personally, they require considerable time, financial resources, and administrative effort. Furthermore, aspiring models without agency representation often struggle to gain exposure to recruiters.

Several online platforms, including Model Mayhem and StarNow, provide digital portfolios and casting notices. These systems improve accessibility compared to traditional recruitment; however, they remain focused primarily on portfolio presentation rather than complete casting management.

21

Critical evaluation of these platforms reveals several limitations:

- Limited support for structured casting workflows.

- Lack of standardized portfolio verification.

- Insufficient search filters for physical characteristics and modeling categories.

- Weak collaboration between recruiters, agencies, photographers, and event organizers.

- Minimal automation during candidate selection.

- Limited algorithm-based matching between casting requirements and candidate profiles.

Consequently, recruiters frequently perform manual candidate comparisons. This can increase recruitment time and make the selection process dependent on individual recruiter effort. A structured matching mechanism can support this process by comparing casting requirements with relevant candidate attributes and producing a ranked list of potentially suitable talents.

#### **2.4 Digital Marketplaces**

Digital marketplace platforms such as LinkedIn, Fiverr, and Upwork have transformed professional networking and freelance employment by enabling individuals to market their skills globally.

These platforms generally include user profile management, job posting, messaging systems, search functions, and reputation mechanisms. According to Lukac and Grow (2021), these components contribute significantly to digital workforce expansion by simplifying employeremployee interactions.

Search and recommendation features are also important within digital marketplaces because users may have difficulty manually reviewing a large number of available profiles or opportunities. Structured filtering and recommendation mechanisms can help users identify relevant results more efficiently.

Despite their success, these platforms are designed for general professional services rather than modeling recruitment. They lack industry-specific capabilities such as:

22

- Digital casting management.

- Professional portfolio verification.

- Appearance-based talent searching.

- Beauty pageant management.

- Fashion event coordination.

- Specialized talent-to-casting matching.

Therefore, while existing digital marketplaces demonstrate the effectiveness of online recruitment, they cannot fully satisfy the operational requirements of modeling and pageant organizations.

#### **2.5 Portfolio-Based Professional Systems**

Digital portfolios have become essential tools for professionals working within creative industries. Unlike traditional résumés, portfolios provide visual evidence of an individual’s skills, experience, achievements, and professional quality.

Within the modeling industry, portfolios commonly include:

- Professional photographs.

- Runway videos.

- Commercial advertisements.

- Physical measurements.

- Awards and achievements.

- Previous modeling experience.

According to Geyik et al. (2018), digital portfolios can support recruiter decision-making by presenting more comprehensive information about candidates and their capabilities.

23

However, many existing portfolio platforms suffer from several weaknesses:

- Lack of standardized portfolio structures.

- Limited support for multimedia content.

- Absence of identity verification.

- Inconsistent presentation formats.

- Difficulty comparing candidates objectively.

These shortcomings can reduce recruitment efficiency and make it difficult for recruiters to compare multiple candidates consistently.

For the proposed system, portfolio information can also contribute to the talent matching process. Candidate attributes such as experience, skills, location, category, and portfolio information can be considered when determining the suitability of a candidate for a particular casting opportunity.

#### **2.6 Multi-Role User Management**

Modern online platforms often serve multiple categories of users, each requiring different permissions and responsibilities. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) is widely adopted to ensure users can only access functions relevant to their assigned roles (Sandhu et al., 1996).

The proposed system supports multiple stakeholders, including:

- Models.

- Modeling agencies.

- Casting directors.

- Pageant organizers.

- Photographers.

- Fashion designers.

- Administrators.

24

Each stakeholder performs distinct activities within the platform. For example, models manage their profiles and portfolios, recruiters publish casting opportunities and evaluate applicants, photographers collaborate with talent, while administrators oversee system operations and verification processes.

Implementing RBAC improves security, protects sensitive information, and enhances overall system organization. It also provides a structured foundation for managing different workflows within a single platform.

#### **2.7 Recommendation and Matching Systems**

Recommendation systems have become fundamental components of modern digital platforms by assisting users in identifying relevant products, services, or opportunities.

Ricci et al. (2015) categorize recommendation techniques into:

- Content-based filtering.

- Collaborative filtering.

- Hybrid recommendation.

Content-based approaches recommend items according to characteristics associated with the user’s requirements or previous preferences. Collaborative approaches use interactions or preferences from multiple users, while hybrid approaches combine different recommendation techniques.

Within talent marketplace platforms, recommendation and matching mechanisms can analyze multiple candidate characteristics, including:

- Physical attributes.

- Professional experience.

- Skills.

- Portfolio quality.

- Geographic location.

- Previous work history.

25

In a modeling and casting environment, matching can be performed by comparing the requirements of a casting opportunity with the characteristics of available talents. For example, a casting opportunity may specify a particular location, age range, height range, experience level, category, or required skills. A matching algorithm can compare these requirements with candidate profiles and determine which candidates satisfy the specified conditions.

A rule-based or weighted matching approach can provide a suitability score based on the degree to which a candidate satisfies the casting requirements. Candidates with higher compatibility can then be presented earlier in the search or recommendation results. This approach can reduce the amount of manual comparison required from recruiters.

However, the use of matching algorithms must be carefully designed because different casting requirements may have different levels of importance. The algorithm should therefore consider the relevant attributes and apply clearly defined matching criteria rather than making unsupported assumptions about candidate suitability.

Although recommendation algorithms have been widely adopted in e-commerce and entertainment platforms, their application within modeling recruitment remains relatively limited. Most existing casting platforms continue to rely heavily on manual searches performed by recruiters.

Therefore, integrating algorithm-based matching into a casting management platform represents an opportunity to improve candidate discovery, reduce recruitment effort, and provide more structured recommendations to recruiters.

#### **2.8 Advanced Search and Filtering in Talent Recruitment**

Search and filtering mechanisms are important components of digital recruitment systems because recruiters may need to evaluate a large number of candidate profiles.

Traditional manual recruitment requires recruiters to individually review candidate information before identifying suitable applicants. Advanced filtering can reduce this workload by allowing recruiters to specify relevant criteria before viewing search results.

26

For a modeling and talent marketplace, relevant filtering criteria may include:

- Country or location.

- Age.

- Height.

- Professional category.

- Experience.

- Skills.

- Portfolio availability.

- Previous work experience.

A structured filtering mechanism can first eliminate candidates who do not satisfy mandatory requirements. The remaining candidates can then be ranked according to additional matching criteria.

Search and filtering therefore complement algorithm-based matching. Filtering can identify candidates who meet basic requirements, while a matching algorithm can further evaluate their overall suitability and generate a ranked result.

This combination can provide recruiters with a more efficient method of discovering suitable talents while maintaining control over the selection criteria.

#### **2.9 Web-Based System Architecture**

Modern web applications typically follow layered client-server architectures that separate the presentation layer, business logic, and database management system.

Pressman and Maxim (2020) argue that layered architectures improve maintainability, scalability, and software quality by separating application responsibilities.

For global talent marketplace systems, scalable architectures are particularly important because they must support:

27

- Large user populations.

- High-resolution image storage.

- Video portfolio management.

- Real-time messaging.

- Concurrent recruiter activities.

- Search and matching operations.

Cloud computing technologies further improve scalability by providing elastic storage and computing resources while reducing infrastructure costs.

A suitable architecture is therefore important for the proposed system because the platform must manage user accounts, multimedia portfolios, casting opportunities, applications, search operations, matching calculations, and communication between different user roles.

#### **2.11 Research Gap**

The literature review identifies several significant research gaps in existing talent marketplace, recruitment, portfolio, and casting management systems.

First, existing platforms do not comprehensively integrate models, agencies, photographers, pageant organizers, and casting directors within a single collaborative global platform. Although general digital marketplaces provide professional networking and recruitment functions, they are not specifically designed to address the requirements of the modeling, fashion, entertainment, and pageant industries.

Second, existing systems provide limited support for structured casting workflow management. A complete casting process may involve publishing a casting opportunity, defining requirements, discovering suitable talents, reviewing applications, comparing candidates, and selecting suitable applicants. These activities are often distributed across different platforms or handled manually.

30

Third, algorithm-based talent matching and recommendation remain underutilized within specialized modeling and casting platforms. Existing systems commonly depend on manual searches and portfolio reviews. There is an opportunity to use structured matching algorithms to compare casting requirements with talent attributes such as location, age, height, experience, skills, category, and portfolio information. This can assist recruiters in identifying and ranking potentially suitable candidates more efficiently.

Fourth, advanced search and filtering are not sufficiently integrated with casting workflows. Recruiters may need to manually search through large numbers of candidate profiles even when specific requirements are available. Combining structured filtering with algorithm-based matching could reduce the number of unsuitable candidates presented to recruiters.

Fifth, portfolio verification and standardized talent classification are insufficiently supported in many existing platforms. This can make candidate evaluation inconsistent and increase the effort required from recruiters when comparing different profiles.

Finally, current platforms primarily address general recruitment, freelance employment, or portfolio presentation and do not fully satisfy the specialized operational requirements of modeling, fashion, entertainment, and pageant industries.

These limitations justify the development of the proposed Global Talent Marketplace and Casting Management System. The proposed system aims to address these gaps by integrating multi-role profiles, multimedia portfolios, casting management, advanced search and filtering, algorithmbased talent matching, basic recommendations, and communication within a unified web-based platform.

#### **5.6 Discussion of Search and Talent Discovery**

Efficient talent discovery is another major requirement of the proposed system.

Recruiters may need to evaluate large numbers of potential candidates based on characteristics such as location, experience, category, age, height, and portfolio information. Manual searching through social media and separate portfolio platforms can increase recruitment time.

The proposed search and filtering functionality addresses this issue by providing a structured method of locating relevant talent.

The successful Search and Filter test indicates that the system was able to display relevant results during testing. This supports the objective of reducing the effort required to identify suitable candidates.

The literature review also identified recommendation and matching systems as an opportunity for improving recruitment efficiency. Content-based, collaborative, and hybrid recommendation techniques have been widely studied in other digital platforms, while their application in modeling recruitment remains more limited.

The current prototype provides basic matching/search functionality rather than a fully developed artificial-intelligence recommendation engine. Therefore, intelligent recommendation remains an opportunity for future development.

#### **5.7 Discussion of Communication**

#### **5.8 Discussion of Security**

Security is particularly important because the proposed system stores user profiles, professional portfolios, applications, and other information.

The implemented security mechanisms include password hashing, JWT authentication, role-based access control, input validation, secure file upload validation, HTTPS communication, session management, and protection against common web vulnerabilities.

The successful role-based access test provides evidence that access restrictions are functioning within the tested prototype.

Nevertheless, security testing in a prototype environment cannot guarantee complete protection against every possible cybersecurity threat. A commercial version would require more comprehensive penetration testing, vulnerability assessment, monitoring, logging, backup strategies, and security auditing.
